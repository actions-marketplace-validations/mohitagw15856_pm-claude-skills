# Syllabus: AI-assisted product work with Agent Skills

**Format:** eight weeks, one 2-hour session a week (a 30-minute input, a 60-minute in-class exercise, a 30-minute critique), plus about four hours of independent work.
**Prerequisites:** none. Week 8 needs a text editor.
**Cost to students:** none. See the free routes in the [module overview](README.md#free-routes-for-running-skills).

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the module a student can:

1. Choose a fitting skill for a product task, and explain why a skill beats a one-off prompt for repeatable work.
2. Write a brief with the facts, constraints and audience a skill needs, and recognise when the brief is the problem.
3. Critique an AI-produced artefact against a professional rubric and redline it into something they would sign.
4. Spot fabricated specifics, unsupported numbers and flattering agreement in AI output.
5. Make and defend product decisions (scope, priority, metrics, launch) with AI as a drafting partner, not a decision maker.
6. Write, validate and test a small skill of their own.

## Assessment

Every assignment is marked with the shared [rubric](rubric.md) (four criteria, scored 0 to 4). Each week's file adds the criteria specific to that artefact.

**What students submit for each assignment:**

1. **The brief:** the exact input they gave the skill.
2. **The kept output:** the skill's draft as they received it (or the run's shared link).
3. **The final artefact,** with their changes visible as a redline or tracked changes.
4. **A reflection of 150 to 250 words:** what the draft got wrong, what they changed, and one thing they would brief differently next time.

The final artefact is the student's work and their responsibility. A fact the AI invented and the student kept is the student's error.

## Policies

- **AI use is required, not just allowed.** The point of the module is to use it well. Students say which route and model they used.
- **No paid keys are required.** A student who has no suitable machine uses a lab machine with Ollama, or the in-browser model.
- **Real data:** students never paste personal data, client data or anything under NDA into a model. The case packs in each week's file are fictional.
- **Late work:** the institution's normal policy applies.
- **Accessibility:** every tool used has a keyboard path. Students who need more time with the in-browser model (which is slower on older laptops) can use a lab machine or the Gemini route.

## Case company

All eight weeks use one fictional company, so the artefacts build on each other:

> **Larder** is a 40-person start-up selling a meal-planning app to busy households in the UK. It has 120,000 monthly active users, 9% of whom pay £4.99 a month. Retention after week 4 has fallen from 41% to 33% over two quarters. The founders believe a "smart shopping list" that syncs with supermarket delivery slots will fix it.

Instructors can swap in their own case. Keep it fictional and give the same facts to every student.
