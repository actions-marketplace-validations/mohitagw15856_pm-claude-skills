# Week 2: Discovery and research

## Learning outcomes

- Plan customer interviews that test a belief rather than confirm it.
- Synthesise interview notes into themes backed by quotes, without inventing evidence.
- Map the assumptions behind a plan and rank them by risk.

## Skills used

- `discovery-interview-guide`: interview plan and question set.
- `user-interview-synthesis`: themes, quotes and implications from notes.
- `assumption-mapper`: hidden assumptions, rated by confidence and impact.

## Case material

Give each group the same six short, fictional interview notes from Larder users (two churned, two at risk, two loyal). Write them before the session; keep them to about 150 words each, with one note that contradicts the founders' belief and one that supports it.

## In-class exercise (60 minutes)

1. **Guide (15 min).** Run `discovery-interview-guide` for "why do households stop using Larder after a month?". Strike out any leading question and rewrite it.
2. **Synthesis (25 min).** Run `user-interview-synthesis` on the six notes. Then check every quote in the output against the notes. Count how many are verbatim, paraphrased, or not in the notes at all.
3. **Assumptions (20 min).** Run `assumption-mapper` on the founders' smart-shopping-list plan. Pick the riskiest assumption and design the cheapest test for it.

## Graded assignment A2: research synthesis and assumption map (10%)

Submit a synthesis of the six notes and an assumption map of the founders' plan, with a one-paragraph recommendation on what to test first.

**Week-specific criteria (0 to 4 each):**

| Criterion | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|
| Evidence fidelity | Every quote is verbatim from the notes; every theme cites at least two notes; the contradicting note is not smoothed away. |
| Risk ranking | The top assumption is both uncertain and decision-changing, and the proposed test could prove it wrong within two weeks. |

## Instructor note

The quote check in step 2 is the most important ten minutes of the module. Models routinely tidy or invent quotes. Students who find one remember it; students who are told about it forget. If no group finds an invented quote, show one you prepared.
