# Week 3: Defining the product

## Learning outcomes

- Write a PRD that a team could build from, with goals, non-goals and measurable success criteria.
- Break a feature into user stories with testable acceptance criteria.
- Use job stories to keep the user's situation, not the feature, at the centre.

## Skills used

- `prd-template`: product requirements document.
- `user-story-writer`: user stories with acceptance criteria.
- `job-story-mapper`: job stories from the user's situation and motivation.

## In-class exercise (60 minutes)

1. **Jobs first (15 min).** Run `job-story-mapper` using the week 2 synthesis as input. Choose the one job the smart shopping list must do well.
2. **PRD (30 min).** Run `prd-template` for the smart shopping list, giving it the chosen job and the week 2 riskiest assumption. Then mark every sentence in the output as F (fact from your brief), A (assumption) or I (invented). Move every A into an "Open questions" section and delete every I.
3. **Stories (15 min).** Run `user-story-writer` on the PRD's first milestone. Swap with another group and try to "pass" a story that should fail its acceptance criteria.

## Graded assignment A3: PRD with user stories (15%)

A PRD for the first release of the smart shopping list, plus the user stories for its first milestone.

**Week-specific criteria (0 to 4 each):**

| Criterion | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|
| Testable success | Success metrics have a baseline, a target and a date, and connect to the retention problem from week 1. |
| Scope discipline | Non-goals are explicit and defended; the first milestone can ship in six weeks with a small team. |
| Acceptance criteria | Every story's criteria are observable and could fail; edge cases (no delivery slot, out-of-stock item) are covered. |

Run the final PRD through [Grade your work](https://mohitagw15856.github.io/pm-claude-skills/grade.html) before submitting and attach the result.

## Instructor note

The F/A/I marking in step 2 is slow the first time and fast by week 6. Insist on it. The common failure this week is a PRD with confident success metrics that have no baseline: ask "where did 15% come from?" until students stop accepting numbers they did not supply.
